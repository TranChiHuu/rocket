      CG and CP: why don't real rockets have big tail fins?

      A model rocket is stable when its centre of gravity (CG) sits above its centre of pressure (CP), usually by 1–2 body diameters, so fins are needed. Big rockets fly mostly above thick air and steer their engines (gimbal), so they stay controlled even with a small margin; the grid fins and flaps only work on the way back down. In this model the margin is negative: with no fins the CP sits above the CG, so the stack is aerodynamically unstable and engine gimbal keeps it pointed.
